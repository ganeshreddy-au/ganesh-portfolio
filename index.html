01 About

Engineering that holds up —
from the transaction to the model.

I'm a backend engineer turned machine learning and data engineer, completing a Master of Data Science at RMIT University in Melbourne.

For two years I worked on the inside of the financial system at Toucan Payments: authorising card payments in under a second, building UPI flows end to end, and keeping fraud out of the pipeline for 100% of card and UPI volume. Every one of those transactions was data, and the fraud checks were only as good as the rules behind them. That's what pulled me towards machine learning.

Python is where most of my ML work lives; Java and SQL are where I learned to build things that can't go down. I'm looking for roles where those two worlds meet.

01 ML · Classification · Personal project

Credit Card Fraud Detection

An end-to-end fraud pipeline on 284,807 real card transactions, where only 0.17% are fraud. A model that always says "genuine" scores 99.8% accuracy and catches nothing — so the work was about finding the rare cases without flooding analysts with false alarms.

What I built

  • EDA surfaced the 99.83 / 0.17 split; a stratified split kept that ratio in testing.
  • Log-transformed and scaled Amount and Time; SMOTE on the training fold only, so no synthetic rows leaked into evaluation.
  • Compared Logistic Regression, Random Forest and Gradient Boosting on ROC-AUC, precision, recall and F1; shipped Random Forest as one serialized pipeline.

Dataset: ULB Credit Card Fraud (Kaggle) — European cardholders, September 2013, PCA-anonymised features.

Why it's data work

  • Reconciliation is a data-matching problem: join two ledgers, explain every mismatch, never lose a rupee.
  • Idempotent processing and explicit state transitions so retries never double-count.
  • The same discipline carries straight into ETL: validate, reconcile, make reruns safe.
